## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Transport Modes and Land Transport
- Network Definition: Transport networks consist of places joined by routes, forming complementary systems of land, water, air, and pipeline modes.
- Roadways: Economical for short distances, offering door-to-door service. Major trans-continental highways link North America, Eurasia, Australia, and Africa.
- Railways: Efficient for heavy bulk freight over long distances. Features major trans-continental routes like the Trans-Siberian, Trans-Canadian, and Orient Express.

### 2. Water Transport and Inland Waterways
- Sea Routes: Dominated by the North Atlantic Big Trunk Route, Mediterranean-Indian Ocean route, and Cape of Good Hope route.
- Shipping Canals: Strategic artificial waterways including the Suez Canal and Panama Canal that dramatically shorten maritime travel times.
- Inland Waterways: Navigable rivers and lakes such as the Rhine, Danube, Volga, Great Lakes, and Mississippi systems.

### 3. Airways, Pipelines, and Communication
- Airways: The fastest and costliest transport mode, concentrated in the Northern Hemisphere with key international hubs.
- Pipelines: Continuous transport of liquids and gases, exemplified by the US Big Inch petroleum pipeline.
- Communication Systems: Spans historical telegraph lines, optic fiber cables, satellite communication, and cyberspace internet networks.

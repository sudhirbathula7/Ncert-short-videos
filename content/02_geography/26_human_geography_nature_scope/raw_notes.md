## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Geography as an Integrative Discipline and Definitions
- Integrative Nature: Geography studies the relationship between the physical environment and life forms, treating Earth as the home of human beings.
- Dualism Debate: Historical discussions balancing nomothetic (law-making) versus idiographic (descriptive) approaches, and regional versus systematic organization.
- Scholarly Definitions: Friedrich Ratzel defines human geography as the synthetic study of human societies and earth surfaces; Ellen Semple emphasizes changing relationships with an unstable earth; Paul Vidal de la Blache focuses on terrestrial synthesis.

### 2. Human-Environment Interplay and Schools of Thought
- Environmental Determinism: Early human societies controlled by nature under low technological development, treating physical environments with awe and necessity.
- Possibilism: Advanced technology and cultural growth allow humans to move from necessity to freedom, creating cultural landscapes that humanize nature.
- Neo-Determinism: Griffith Taylor's middle path (stop-and-go determinism) advocating that humans conquer nature by obeying its ecological limits to prevent crises like global warming.

### 3. Historical Evolution and Interdisciplinary Sub-Fields
- Historical Stages: Moving from colonial exploration and regional analysis to areal differentiation, the quantitative revolution of the 1960s, and 1970s humanistic/radical schools.
- Modern Schools: Welfare and humanistic approaches focusing on social well-being, radical approaches using Marxian theory, and behavioural approaches examining lived spatial experiences.
- Sub-Fields: Interfacing human geography fields (social, urban, political, economic, population, settlement) with sociology, demography, economics, and political science.

## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Mineral Characteristics and Distribution Belts
- Mineral Definition: Naturally occurring organic or inorganic substances with definite physical and chemical properties, predominantly found in peninsular crystalline rocks.
- Distribution Belts: Grouped into the North-Eastern Plateau belt (Chhotanagpur, Odisha), South-Western Plateau belt (Karnataka, Goa, Kerala), and North-Western belt (Aravali region).
- General Properties: Spatial unevenness, inverse relationship between quality and quantity, and exhaustibility over time, necessitating strict conservation.

### 2. Metallic and Energy Mineral Reserves
- Ferrous and Non-Ferrous: Extensive iron ore and manganese reserves contrasting with a poor endowment of non-ferrous metals, except for bauxite.
- Fossil Fuels: Coal reserves split between Gondwana and tertiary formations; petroleum tapped in Assam, Gujarat, Mumbai High offshore fields, and newer east-coast basins.
- Atomic Minerals: Uranium sourced from Dharwar rocks along the Singhbhum belt, and thorium extracted from coastal monazite sands in Kerala, Odisha, and Andhra Pradesh.

### 3. Non-Conventional Energy and Conservation
- Renewable Sources: Transitioning toward solar photovoltaics, wind energy farms, tidal potential, geothermal steam at Manikaran, and municipal bio-energy projects.
- Conservation Framework: Safeguarding exhaustible assets through the development of renewable alternatives, scrap metal recycling, strategic substitutes, and export restrictions.

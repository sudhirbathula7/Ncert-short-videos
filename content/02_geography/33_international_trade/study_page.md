# High-Yield Revision Sheet: International Trade

# International Trade
*Subject: Geography & Global Economics | NCERT Class 12 Ch. 8 | UPSC & State PSC Core*

---

### ⚡ 30-Second Snapshot
- **International trade** is the voluntary exchange of goods and services across national boundaries, driven by uneven resource distribution and regional specialization.
- Historical milestones span ancient pathways like the **Silk Route**, colonial trade, and post-Industrial Revolution globalization.
- Trade balances track imports versus exports, yielding **favorable (positive)** or **unfavorable (negative)** balances.
- The **World Trade Organisation (WTO)** governs global trade rules, succeeding GATT in 1995.
- **Regional trade blocs** unite geographically proximate nations, generating over half of global trade volume.
- **Maritime ports** act as primary trade gateways, classified by cargo type, inland location, and specialized operational functions.

---

### 📐 Trade Mechanics and Institutional Frameworks

- **Basis of International Trade:**
  - Uneven distribution of physical resources, mineral deposits, and climatic conditions.
  - Population factors, cultural crafts, stages of economic development, and foreign investment.
- **Global Institutions and Agreements:**
  - **Bilateral vs Multilateral Trade:** Two-country agreements versus multi-nation frameworks.
  - **WTO Structure:** Headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland; resolves trade disputes, covers services, and manages intellectual property rights.

---

### ⚠️ Common UPSC Exam Traps
- **Trap 1 (Trade Balance Direction):** A favorable (positive) balance of trade occurs when export earnings exceed import expenditures, not the reverse.
- **Trap 2 (Entrepôt Function):** Entrepôt ports are not simple domestic consumption terminals; they function as collection and re-export hubs for goods from multiple countries.
- **Trap 3 (GATT to WTO Succession):** The WTO replaced GATT in 1995 as a permanent international institution, whereas GATT was an interim agreement established in 1948.
- **Trap 4 (Inland Port Location):** Inland ports are situated inland along navigable rivers or canals rather than directly on ocean coastlines.

### 📝 Prelims Practice Challenge

**Q1. Consider the following statements regarding international trade foundations:**
1. A favorable balance of trade occurs when the total value of a country's imports exceeds the value of its exports.
2. Uneven global resource distribution and regional specialization form the fundamental basis of international trade.
3. Entrepôt ports function as specialized collection centers where goods arrive from multiple countries for re-export.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(B) 2 and 3 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is incorrect because a favorable trade balance requires exports to exceed imports. Statements 2 and 3 are correct descriptions of the basis of trade and entrepôt port functions.

**Q2. Consider the following statements regarding global trade institutions and ports:**
1. The World Trade Organisation (WTO) was established on January 1, 1995, succeeding the earlier GATT framework.
2. Inland ports like Kolkata are situated directly on ocean coastlines without any river or canal connection.
3. Regional trade blocs generate more than half of the total volume of world trade.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(C) 1 and 3 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statements 1 and 3 are correct because the WTO succeeded GATT in 1995, and regional trade blocs account for roughly fifty-two percent of world trade. Statement 2 is incorrect because inland ports are located away from sea coasts, linked via navigable rivers or canals (such as Kolkata on the Hooghly branch of the Ganga).

### 🖋️ Mains Practice Question (10 Marks / 150 Words)

**Question:** *"Examine the evolution of international trade from historical routes to modern multilateral institutions. What are the major criticisms leveled against organizations like the World Trade Organisation in the context of developing nations?"*

#### 💡 Structured Answer Framework:
- **Introduction (25–30 words):**  
  Define international trade as a vital driver of global economic interdependence, evolving from ancient overland corridors to complex multilateral systems.
- **Body Arguments (80–90 words):**  
  - *Historical Evolution:* Transition from the Silk Route and colonial commerce to post-Industrial Revolution specialization and global market integration.
  - *Institutional Governance:* Establishment of GATT and its replacement by the WTO in 1995 to enforce rules, reduce tariffs, and arbitrate trade disputes.
  - *Criticisms from Developing Nations:* Asymmetric market access, protectionist agricultural subsidies in developed countries, and concerns over intellectual property rights and environmental standards.
- **Conclusion / Way Forward (25–30 words):**  
  Reforming multilateral trade institutions to ensure equitable rules and fair market access is essential for fostering inclusive global economic growth.

## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Water Endowment and Demand
- Resource Mismatch: India faces a stark mismatch, holding 4 percent of world water resources for over 17 percent of the global population.
- Utilisable Limit: Out of total precipitation and river flow, only about 1,122 cubic kilometers is utilisable due to hydrological and topographical constraints.
- Sectoral Dominance: Agriculture accounts for over 89 percent of surface water and 92 percent of groundwater use, driven by spatio-temporal rainfall variability and the Green Revolution.

### 2. Water Quality and Pollution
- Contamination Sources: Industrial, agricultural, and domestic effluents drive organic and bacterial contamination in Indian rivers.
- Hotspots: Severely polluted stretches found along the Yamuna, Sabarmati, Gomti, and Ganga rivers, alongside heavy metal and fluoride/arsenic groundwater contamination.
- Legal Framework: Governed by the Water Prevention and Control of Pollution Act 1974 and the Environment Protection Act 1986.

### 3. Conservation, Management, and Policy
- Watershed Management: Integrated conservation and regeneration of land and water resources, highlighted by successful community models like Ralegan Siddhi.
- Rainwater Harvesting: Low-cost rooftop and traditional underground storage (Tanka/Kund) to recharge aquifers and bridge urban demand-supply gaps.
- National Policies: National Water Policy 2012 prioritizing water as an economic good and Jal Kranti Abhiyan 2015-16 ensuring community-led water security.

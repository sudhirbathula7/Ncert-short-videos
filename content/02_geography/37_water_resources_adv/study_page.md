# Water Resources (Advanced)
*Subject: Indian Geography & Environmental Management | NCERT Class 12 (People & Economy) Ch. 4 | UPSC & State PSC Core*

---

### ⚡ 30-Second Snapshot
- Water is a cyclic resource, but **freshwater accounts for only ~3%** globally, creating severe spatial and temporal scarcity.
- India faces a stark **resource-population mismatch**: holding ~4% of world water resources for >17% of the global population.
- Out of total precipitation and river flow, only **~1,122 cubic km (60%) is utilisable**.
- **Agriculture dominates sectoral water demand**, consuming ~89% of surface water and ~92% of groundwater.
- Over-extraction of groundwater causes severe water table decline and **fluoride/arsenic contamination** in regional aquifers.
- Water conservation relies on **watershed management, rainwater harvesting, traditional storage (Tanka/Kund), and community initiatives** (Ralegan Siddhi).

### 📐 Water Quality and Pollution Frameworks

- **Major Water Quality Issues:**
  - **Biological Contamination:** Organic waste and municipal sewage drive bacterial pollution, peaking during summer low-flow periods.
  - **Severe Stretches:** Heavily polluted river segments identified along the Yamuna (Delhi-Etawah), Sabarmati (Ahmedabad), and Ganga (Kanpur-Varanasi).
  - **Chemical Toxicity:** Excessive groundwater drafting releases heavy metals, nitrates, and toxic fluoride/arsenic into drinking water supplies.
- **Legal and Policy Instruments:**
  - *Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974* and *Environment Protection Act, 1986*.
  - *National Water Policy, 2012:* Treats water as an economic good, promotes water footprint benchmarking, and advocates a national water framework law.

### ⚠️ Common UPSC Exam Traps
- **Trap 1 (Total vs Utilisable Water):** Total surface flow is 1,869 cubic km, but due to hydrological and topographical limits, only 1,122 cubic km is utilisable.
- **Trap 2 (Basin Distribution Evenness):** Surface water resources are not evenly spread; the Ganga, Brahmaputra, and Barak basins hold 60 percent of surface water despite covering a third of India's land.
- **Trap 3 (Sectoral Demand Ranking):** Agricultural irrigation consumes the vast majority of water (~90%), vastly outstripping domestic and industrial demand.
- **Trap 4 (Watershed Success Prerequisite):** Watershed management programmes cannot succeed through top-down government fiat alone; community participation is the essential prerequisite for sustainability.

### 📝 Prelims Practice Challenge

**Q1. Consider the following statements regarding India's water resource endowment:**
1. India holds roughly four percent of the world's water resources for over seventeen percent of the global population.
2. The entire annual surface flow of 1,869 cubic kilometers can be fully utilised for irrigation and industry without hydrological constraints.
3. The Ganga, Brahmaputra, and Barak river basins account for sixty percent of India's total surface water resources.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(C) 1 and 3 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statements 1 and 3 are correct because India faces a resource-population mismatch, and the Ganga-Brahmaputra-Barak basins hold 60% of surface water. Statement 2 is incorrect because only about 60% (1,122 cubic km) of surface flow is utilisable due to topographical and hydrological limits.

**Q2. Consider the following statements regarding water conservation and quality in India:**
1. Agricultural irrigation consumes the largest share of both surface water and groundwater resources in India.
2. Traditional underground storage tanks known as *Tanks* or *Kunds* are widely used in Rajasthan for rainwater harvesting.
3. The Central Pollution Control Board finds that industrial and domestic waste contamination peaks during the monsoon season due to high river flow.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(A) 1 and 2 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statements 1 and 2 are correct because agriculture dominates water use, and Tankas/Kunds are traditional Rajasthani harvesting structures. Statement 3 is incorrect because organic and bacterial contamination concentrations peak during *summer* when river flow is low, not during the monsoon.

### 🖋️ Mains Practice Question (10 Marks / 150 Words)

**Question:** *"Analyze the challenges of water scarcity and quality degradation in India. How can decentralized conservation models and integrated watershed management ensure sustainable water security?"*

#### 💡 Structured Answer Framework:
- **Introduction (25–30 words):**  
  Highlight India's acute resource-population mismatch in water endowment (~4% water for >17% population) and the escalating crisis of scarcity and pollution.
- **Body Arguments (80–90 words):**  
  - *Scarcity and Sectoral Pressures:* Heavy dependence on agricultural irrigation leading to severe groundwater over-extraction and aquifer depletion.
  - *Quality Degradation:* Industrial and municipal pollution along major river stretches alongside toxic fluoride and arsenic contamination.
  - *Decentralized Conservation Solutions:* Implementing rainwater harvesting, traditional storage, and participatory watershed management (such as Ralegan Siddhi) to recharge aquifers.
- **Conclusion / Way Forward (25–30 words):**  
  Transitioning toward demand-side management, strict pollution enforcement, and community-led conservation is vital for achieving long-term national water security.

### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: Longitudes & Indian Standard Time
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Isometric cutaway showing meridians running from pole to pole, with the Prime Meridian at Greenwich and the 82.5-degree East Indian Standard Meridian highlighted with a sweeping clock dial. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Unlike latitudes, all meridians of longitude are equal in length, converging at the poles. The Earth rotates three hundred sixty degrees in twenty-four hours, which equals fifteen degrees every hour, or one degree every four minutes. India aligns its clocks to eighty-two point five degrees East, placing Indian Standard Time five and a half hours ahead of GMT!"

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: Rotation vs. Circle of Illumination
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Cross-section showing the Earth spinning west-to-east on its 23.5-degree tilted axis, with the Circle of Illumination cleanly splitting day and night across the continents. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Daily life is governed by rotation: spinning west-to-east every twenty-four hours. Notice this critical exam detail: the Circle of Illumination that divides day from night does not match the tilted rotational axis! Without rotation, one half of the Earth would scorch while the other froze permanently."

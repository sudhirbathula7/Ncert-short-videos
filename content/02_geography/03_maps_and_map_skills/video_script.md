### [0:00 - 0:14] Page 1: Why Maps Beat Globes
**Visual**: Clean off-white canvas. Left 60%: Architectural isometric cutaway showing a round desk globe flattening into a crisp, multi-layered regional map sheet detailing streets and rivers. Right 40%: Structured editorial typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:

### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: The Large vs. Small Scale Paradox
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Isometric cutaway comparing two magnifying lenses: one over a continental map of India marked Small Scale, the other zooming into a detailed village layout marked Large Scale. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Here is a classic exam trap: scale! A small scale map covers huge areas like continents, but gives very little detail. A large scale map zooms into a single village or neighborhood, revealing houses, roads, and land plots with sharp, high-resolution precision."

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: Directions, Bearings & Standard Colors
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Cross-section showing a brass magnetic compass needle aligning North alongside the four Cardinal points, with standard terrain colors: blue water, brown peaks, yellow plateaus, and green plains. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Orientation starts with the North line. The four Cardinal points—North, South, East, and West—guide your main directions, while intermediate vectors add precision. Cartographers use universal colors: blue for water, brown for mountains, yellow for plateaus, and green for lowland plains."

## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. What is an Industry and Classification
- Secondary Activities: Manufacturing transforms raw materials into more valuable products, adding utility at each stage.
- Classification by Raw Material: Agro-based, mineral-based, marine-based, and forest-based industries.
- Classification by Size and Ownership: Cottage and household industries, small-scale units, large-scale plants, along with private, public, joint, and cooperative sectors.
- Industrial System: Comprises inputs (raw materials, labor, power, transport), processes (manufacturing operations), and outputs (finished goods and income).

### 2. Location Factors and Industrial Disasters
- Location Determinants: Availability of raw materials, land, water, labor, power, capital, transport, and markets.
- Industrial Regions: Concentrations of industries sharing proximity benefits, such as the Mumbai-Pune cluster, Hugli region, and Chotanagpur belt in India.
- Industrial Disasters: Technical failures or hazardous material handling, exemplified by the Bhopal gas tragedy involving methyl isocyanate and the Gao Qiao gas well blowout in China.

### 3. Major Industries: Iron and Steel and Textiles
- Iron and Steel: Feeder industry known as the backbone of modern manufacturing. Involves smelting ore in blast furnaces. Key centers include Jamshedpur (TISCO) and Pittsburgh (using Great Lakes transport).
- Cotton Textile Industry: Uses natural and man-made fibers. Pioneers in mechanization during the Industrial Revolution. Key hubs include Ahmedabad in India and Osaka in Japan.
- Sunrise Industries: Emerging growth sectors such as information technology, wellness, hospitality, and knowledge services.

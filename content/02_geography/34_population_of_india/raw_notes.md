## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Distribution and Density
- Scale and Census: India's 2011 population reached 1,210 million, tracked via decennial censuses beginning fully in 1881.
- Uneven Distribution: Highly concentrated across major states like Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra, driven by physical terrain, water, and historical settlement patterns.
- Density Metrics: Crude density reached 382 persons per square kilometer in 2011. Specialized indices include physiological density (population over cultivated area) and agricultural density (agricultural population over cultivable area).

### 2. Population Growth Phases
- Phase I (1901-1921): Stagnant or stationary growth with high birth and death rates.
- Phase II (1921-1951): Steady growth driven by falling mortality and constant fertility.
- Phase III (1951-1981): Population explosion marked by rapid mortality decline and high fertility averaging 2.2 percent annual growth.
- Phase IV (Post-1981): Slowing growth driven by declining crude birth rates and rising female education.

### 3. Composition and Occupational Structure
- Rural-Urban Split: Roughly 68.8 percent rural residing across over six lakh villages, alongside a growing urban population of 31.16 percent.
- Linguistic and Religious Diversity: Four major language families (Indo-European, Dravidian, Austric, Sino-Tibetan) and diverse religious demographics led by Hindus and Muslims.
- Workforce Composition: Work participation rate at 39.8 percent in 2011, with 54.6 percent engaged in primary agricultural activities, showing a gradual sectoral shift toward secondary and tertiary work.

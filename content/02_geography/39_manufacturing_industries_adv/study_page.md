# Planning & Sustainable Development in Indian Context
*Subject: Indian Geography & Regional Planning | NCERT Class 12 (People & Economy) Ch. 6 | UPSC & State PSC Core*

---

### ⚡ 30-Second Snapshot
- **Economic planning** in India transitioned from centralized sectoral and regional planning under the Planning Commission to **NITI Aayog (established January 1, 2015)**.
- **Target area planning** was introduced to correct worsening regional imbalances via specialized programmes like the Drought Prone Area Programme (DPAP).
- The **Integrated Tribal Development Project (ITDP)** in Bharmaur successfully improved infrastructure and raised Gaddi female literacy from 1.88% (1971) to 65% (2011).
- **Sustainable development** gained global prominence via the **1987 Brundtland Report ("Our Common Future")**, defining it as meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
- The **Indira Gandhi Canal Command Area** successfully "greened" the Thar Desert, but suffered severe ecological backlash from **waterlogging and soil salinity**.
- Restorative sustainability measures include **strict water management, cropping pattern control, afforestation, and the warabandi distribution system**.

---

### 🏛️ Planning Approaches & Case Studies Grid

| Programme / Case Study | Core Objective & Institutional Focus | Regional Impact & Strategic Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Sectoral Planning** | Formulating economic development schemes across specific sectors | Addresses broad national sectors like agriculture, transport, and energy. |
| **Regional Planning** | Adopting a spatial perspective to reduce regional imbalances | Focuses on lagging geographic regions to equalize economic growth. |
| **DPAP Programme** | Drought Prone Area Programme initiated in the 4th Plan | Shifted from civil works to integrated watershed development for ecological balance. |
| **Bharmour ITDP** | Tribal sub-plan for the Gaddi community in Himachal Pradesh | Improved literacy, sex ratios, and basic infrastructure in a backward hill region. |
| **Indira Gandhi Canal** | Massive irrigation project transforming the Thar Desert | Boosted agricultural output via Stage-I and Stage-II, creating waterlogging challenges. |
| **Warabandi System** | Equitable canal water distribution within command outlets | Promotes efficient water conveyance and curbs excessive field flooding. |

---

### 📐 Evolution of Development and Sustainability

- **Evolution of the "Development" Concept:**
  - *Post-WWII:* Defined narrowly as economic growth measured via GNP and per capita income.
  - *1970s:* Shifted toward equity, recognizing that high growth often coexisted with poverty ("redistribution with growth").
  - *1980s Onward:* Broadened into social and material well-being encompassing health, education, and civil rights.
- **Core Principles of Sustainable Development:**
  - Integrates ecological, social, and economic dimensions of present actions while conserving resources for future generations.

---

### ⚠️ Common UPSC Exam Traps
- **Trap 1 (Planning Institution Continuity):** The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog on January 1, 2015, shifting from central planning to cooperative federalism.
- **Trap 2 (DPAP Initial Focus):** The Drought Prone Area Programme initially focused heavily on labour-intensive civil works before shifting to integrated watershed development.
- **Trap 3 (Indira Gandhi Canal Ecological Impact):** While the canal successfully greened the desert, excessive irrigation caused severe negative ecological outcomes, notably waterlogging and soil salinity.
- **Trap 4 (Bharmour Tribal Community):** The Bharmaur ITDP specifically targets the Gaddi tribal community practicing transhumance in Himachal Pradesh.

### 📝 Prelims Practice Challenge

**Q1. Consider the following statements regarding planning institutions and approaches in India:**
1. Sectoral planning focuses on reducing regional imbalances through spatial geographic strategies.
2. The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog on January 1, 2015.
3. Target area programmes were introduced when planning experience showed that regional disparities were worsening.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(B) 2 and 3 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is incorrect because *regional* planning focuses on spatial imbalances, whereas *sectoral* planning focuses on economic sectors like agriculture and industry. Statements 2 and 3 are correct because NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015, and target area programmes were designed to correct worsening regional disparities.

**Q2. Consider the following statements regarding the Indira Gandhi Canal Command Area and sustainable development:**
1. The Brundtland Report ("Our Common Future") was published in 1987.
2. Excessive canal irrigation in the Thar Desert has successfully eliminated all ecological challenges without causing any waterlogging.
3. The warabandi system is designed to ensure equitable distribution of canal water within a command area outlet.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(C) 1 and 3 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statements 1 and 3 are correct because the Brundtland Report was published in 1987, and warabandi ensures equitable canal water distribution. Statement 2 is incorrect because excessive irrigation caused severe negative ecological impacts, notably waterlogging and soil salinity.

### 🖋️ Mains Practice Question (10 Marks / 150 Words)

**Question:** *"Examine the rationale behind target area planning in India. Using the Indira Gandhi Canal Command Area as an example, discuss the environmental trade-offs of large-scale irrigation and measures for ecological restoration."*

#### 💡 Structured Answer Framework:
- **Introduction (25–30 words):**  
  Define economic planning in India and explain that spatial disparities necessitated target area planning to address lagging regions.
- **Body Arguments (80–90 words):**  
  - *Rationale of Target Area Planning:* Programs like DPAP and Bharmaur ITDP target backward regions to bridge socioeconomic gaps.
  - *Indira Gandhi Canal Impacts:* Transformed the Thar Desert ecology, boosting agricultural production of wheat and cotton.
  - *Environmental Trade-Offs:* Excessive water application and poor drainage resulted in severe waterlogging and soil salinity.
  - *Restorative Measures:* Strict water management, cropping pattern regulation, afforestation, and the warabandi distribution system.
- **Conclusion / Way Forward (25–30 words):**  
  Balancing agricultural productivity with ecological sustainability through integrated watershed management is vital for long-term regional development.

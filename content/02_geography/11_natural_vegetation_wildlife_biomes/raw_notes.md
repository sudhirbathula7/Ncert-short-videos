## Part 1: World Natural Vegetation

Natural vegetation depends primarily on **temperature and moisture** (climate), and secondarily on **slope** and **soil thickness**. As climate changes with **altitude** and **latitude**, vegetation type changes accordingly — this is why a journey from a mountain's foothills to its peak, or from the equator to the poles, passes through many vegetation zones.

**Three broad categories of natural vegetation:**
| Category | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Forests** | Grow where temperature and rainfall are plentiful (dense or open, depending on degree) |
| **Grasslands** | Grow in regions of moderate rainfall |
| **Shrubs** | Thorny shrubs/scrub grow in dry regions |

### Types of Forests
| Forest Type | Location | Characteristics | Key Trees | Common Animals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical Evergreen (Rainforest)** | Near the equator/close to tropics | Hot, heavy rain year-round, no dry season → trees don't shed leaves; dense canopy blocks sunlight | Rosewood, ebony, mahogany (hardwood) | Anaconda and many others (extremely high biodiversity — the Amazon rainforest is called "the lungs of the earth") |
| **Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon forest)** | Large parts of India, N. Australia, Central America | Seasonal climate; trees shed leaves in the dry season to conserve water | Sal, teak, neem, shisham (hardwood, useful for furniture/construction) | Tigers, lions, elephants, langurs, monkeys |
| **Temperate Evergreen** | Mid-latitude coastal regions, mainly eastern continental margins (SE USA, S. China, SE Brazil) | Mix of hard and soft wood | Oak, pine, eucalyptus | — |
| **Temperate Deciduous** | Higher latitudes (NE USA, China, New Zealand, Chile, W. Europe coasts) | Shed leaves in the dry season | Oak, ash, beech | Deer, foxes, wolves; birds like pheasants, monals |
| **Mediterranean Vegetation** | Around the Mediterranean Sea; also California, SW Africa, SW South America, SW Australia | Hot dry summers, mild rainy winters; trees have thick bark & wax-coated leaves to reduce transpiration; "Orchards of the world" | Citrus fruits (oranges, figs, olives, grapes) — mostly cultivated, natural vegetation largely removed | Limited wildlife |
| **Coniferous Forest (Taiga)** | High latitudes (50°–70°N), also high altitudes | Tall, softwood, evergreen trees; wood used for pulp/paper/newsprint, matchboxes, packing boxes. *"Taiga" means "pure/untouched" in Russian* | Chir, pine, cedar | Silver fox, mink, polar bear |

### Types of Grasslands
| Grassland type | Location | Characteristics | Regional names | Animals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical grasslands** | Either side of the equator, extending to the tropics | Moderate-to-low rainfall; grass can grow 3–4 m tall (e.g., African Savannah) | E. Africa: **Savanna**; Brazil: **Campos**; Venezuela: **Llanos** | Elephants, zebras, giraffes, deer, leopards |
| **Temperate grasslands** | Mid-latitude zones, continental interiors | Short, nutritious grass | Argentina: **Pampas**; N. America: **Prairie**; S. Africa: **Veld**; C. Asia: **Steppe**; Australia: **Downs** | Wild buffaloes, bisons, antelopes |

### Other Vegetation Types
- **Thorny bushes/desert vegetation**: found in tropical deserts on continents' western margins; scarce vegetation cover due to scanty rain and intense heat.
- **Tundra vegetation**: found in polar regions of Europe, Asia, North America — extremely cold, very limited plant growth (mosses, lichens, small shrubs, growing only during the short summer). Animals here have thick fur/skin for cold protection: seal, walrus, musk-ox, Arctic owl, polar bear, snow fox.

### Human Impact
Clearing forests for agriculture and settlement threatens wildlife habitats, the future availability of oxygen, water vapour, timber, fruits, and nuts, and biodiversity generally — a reminder that vegetation loss has cascading consequences.

## Part 2: Biodiversity and Conservation (Class 11)

### What is Biodiversity?
**Biodiversity** ("bio" = life + "diversity" = variety) = the number and variety of organisms (plants, animals, micro-organisms), the genes they contain, and the ecosystems they form, within a specified region. It is the product of **2.5–3.5 billion years of evolution**.

- Areas rich in solar energy and water input tend to have the widest biodiversity — biodiversity is **consistently richer in the tropics**, decreasing toward the poles.
- Estimated global species count: 2 million to 100 million, best estimate ~10 million; many species remain unclassified (e.g., ~40% of South American freshwater fish).
- The average species "half-life" is 1–4 million years; **99% of species that ever lived are now extinct**.

### Three Levels of Biodiversity
| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Genetic diversity** | Variation of genes *within* a species (e.g., humans — all *Homo sapiens* — vary in height, colour, appearance); essential for healthy breeding populations |
| **Species diversity** | Variety/number of species in a defined area; areas especially rich in this are called **hotspots** of diversity |
| **Ecosystem diversity** | Differences between ecosystem types and the diversity of habitats/processes within each; ecosystem boundaries are not rigidly defined, making this hard to demarcate |

### Importance of Biodiversity — Three Roles
1. **Ecological role**: every species contributes to ecosystem functioning — capturing/storing energy, producing/decomposing organic material, cycling water and nutrients, fixing atmospheric gases, regulating climate. More diverse ecosystems are more resilient/productive and better able to adapt to environmental change.
2. **Economic role**: biodiversity is a resource base for food, pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics; **crop diversity (agro-biodiversity)** is a key subset. Key commodities: food crops, livestock, forest products, fish, medicinal resources.
3. **Scientific role**: each species offers clues about how life evolved and functions; understanding this is key to understanding — and sustaining — the ecosystems humans are also part of. There is also an **ethical dimension**: every species has an intrinsic right to exist, so deliberately causing extinction is considered morally wrong.

### Loss of Biodiversity
Causes:
- Rising human population → higher resource consumption → habitat/species loss, especially in the **tropics** (which hold ~1/4 of world land area but ~3/4 of world population, and ~50% of the planet's species, in tropical rainforests).
- Natural calamities: earthquakes, floods, volcanic eruptions, forest fires, droughts.
- **Pollutants**: pesticides, hydrocarbons, toxic heavy metals — kill weak/sensitive species.
- **Exotic species**: non-native species introduced into an ecosystem, which can severely damage the natural biotic community.
- **Poaching**: hunting of tigers, elephants, rhinoceroses, crocodiles, minks, birds for horns/tusks/hides — pushing species toward endangerment.

### IUCN Threatened Species Categories
The **International Union of Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources (IUCN)** classifies threatened species into:
| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Endangered** | In danger of extinction; tracked via the IUCN's **Red List** |
| **Vulnerable** | Likely to become endangered soon if current threats continue |
| **Rare** | Small global population, confined to limited areas or thinly scattered |

### Conservation of Biodiversity
Because all life forms are interlinked, disturbance to one species destabilizes others — potentially threatening human existence too.

**World Conservation Strategy** (agreed steps):
1. Preserve endangered species.
2. Prevent extinction via proper planning/management.
3. Preserve varieties of food crops, forage plants, timber trees, livestock, and their wild relatives.
4. Identify and protect habitats of wild relatives.
5. Safeguard habitats where species feed, breed, rest, and raise young.
6. Regulate international trade in wild plants and animals.

**India's steps**: the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** — established national parks, sanctuaries, and biosphere reserves.

- **Mega-diversity centres**: 12 tropical countries holding a huge share of world species diversity — **Mexico, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Brazil, Democratic Republic of Congo, Madagascar, China, India, Malaysia, Indonesia, Australia.**
- **Biodiversity hotspots**: areas identified by the IUCN as especially vulnerable, defined by their vegetation (since plants determine an ecosystem's primary productivity). E.g., in **Madagascar**, ~85% of plants/animals are found nowhere else on Earth; **Hawaii's** unique species are threatened by introduced species and land development.
- **Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro (June 1992)**: India and 155 other nations signed the **Convention on Biodiversity**, committing to conservation with sustainable use — requiring cooperation of local communities and strong local institutions, since ongoing conservation *process* matters as much as protecting individual species or habitats.

| Mediterranean vegetation | Vegetation adapted to hot dry summers & mild rainy winters |
| Taiga (Coniferous forest) | High-latitude/altitude softwood evergreen forest |
| Savanna / Prairie / Steppe / Pampas / Veld / Downs | Regional names for tropical/temperate grasslands |
| Tundra | Cold polar vegetation zone (mosses, lichens, small shrubs) |
| Biodiversity | Variety of life (genes, species, ecosystems) in a region |
| Genetic / Species / Ecosystem diversity | The three levels at which biodiversity is measured |
| Hotspot | Area especially rich in species diversity / especially vulnerable to biodiversity loss |
| Exotic species | Non-native species introduced into an ecosystem |
| Endangered / Vulnerable / Rare species | IUCN threat categories |
| Red List | IUCN's list of globally endangered species |
| Mega-diversity centres | 12 tropical countries with the world's richest species diversity |
| Earth Summit (1992) | UN conference where the Convention on Biodiversity was signed |

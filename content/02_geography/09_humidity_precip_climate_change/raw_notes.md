## Core Factual & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Water in the Atmosphere & Humidity Mechanics
- States of Water: Exists in gaseous, liquid, and solid forms, cycling continuously via evaporation, transpiration, condensation, and precipitation.
- Absolute Humidity: The actual mass or weight of water vapour present in a unit volume of air, expressed in grams per cubic meter (g/m³). Varies by location because holding capacity depends directly on temperature.
- Relative Humidity: The percentage ratio of moisture present in the air compared to its full holding capacity at a specific temperature. It is generally highest over oceans and lowest over continental interiors.
- Saturated Air: Air that holds water vapour to its absolute maximum capacity at a given temperature and cannot absorb additional moisture.
- Dew Point: The precise temperature threshold to which a given air sample must be cooled to achieve complete saturation.

### 2. Evaporation, Condensation & Micro-Meteorological Forms
- Evaporation & Latent Heat: Liquid converts to gas driven by heat and **latent heat of vaporisation** (energy required to convert liquid to vapour without a temperature change). Increased temperature and active air movement accelerate evaporation.
- Condensation Catalysts: Gas converts back to liquid through heat loss, occurring around microscopic **hygroscopic condensation nuclei** (dust, smoke, ocean salt particles). Temperature drop is the primary catalyst.
- Micro-Meteorological Condensation Forms:
  - Dew: Droplets deposited on cool solid surfaces when clear skies, calm air, high humidity, and long cold nights coincide, with a dew point **above freezing**.
  - Frost: Similar to dew, but occurring when the dew point is **at or below freezing**, causing moisture to form direct ice crystals.
  - Fog: Suspended water droplets formed by a sudden temperature drop in moist air near the surface, reducing visibility to **less than 1 km**.
  - Mist: Similar to fog but with higher moisture content per nucleus, yielding a visibility range of **1 to 2 km**.
  - Smog: Industrial smoke blended with ground fog.
- Cloud Classification Architectures:
  - Cirrus: High-altitude (8,000 to 12,000 m), thin, feathery, white ice crystal formations.
  - Cumulus: Mid-altitude (4,000 to 7,000 m), cotton-wool-like, patchy formations with flat bases.
  - Stratus: Layered sheet clouds covering extensive sky areas.
  - Nimbus: Mid-level or near-surface dark grey, dense, opaque rain-bearing clouds.

### 3. Precipitation Types and Genesis
- Forms of Precipitation: Liquid rainfall and solid snowfall (hexagonal ice crystals when temperature is below 0°C), alongside sleet (refrozen melted snow via sub-freezing surface layers) and multi-layered ice hail.
- Rainfall Mechanics by Origin:
  - Convectional Rain: Heated air expands and rises rapidly, cooling and condensing into cumulus clouds producing short, heavy showers accompanied by thunder and lightning (common in equatorial regions and continental interiors).
  - Orographic (Relief) Rain: Saturated air forced upward over mountain barriers cools and condenses, delivering heavy rain on windward slopes while leaving leeward slopes dry (**rain-shadow areas**).
  - Cyclonic (Frontal) Rain: Associated with extra-tropical weather fronts and mid-latitude cyclones.
- Global Distribution Regimes: Rainfall generally decreases from the equator toward the poles. Coastal regions receive more precipitation than continental interiors, and oceans receive more than landmasses. Major annual precipitation regimes range from over 200 cm in equatorial belts to under 50 cm in rain-shadow and high-latitude interiors.

### 4. Koeppen’s Empirical Climate Classification
- Framework: Developed by V. Koeppen based on mean annual and monthly temperature and precipitation thresholds closely correlated with regional natural vegetation distribution.
- Five Major Climatic Groups:
  - Group A (Tropical): Coldest month average temperature at or above 18°C (includes Af wet, Am monsoon, and Aw savanna sub-types).
  - Group B (Dry): Potential evaporation exceeds total precipitation (includes subtropical and mid-latitude steppes BS and deserts BW).
  - Group C (Warm Temperate): Coldest month average between -3°C and 18°C (includes Cs Mediterranean, Cfa humid subtropical, and Cfb marine west coast).
  - Group D (Cold Snow Forest): Coldest month average at or below -3°C (includes humid Df and dry-winter Dw sub-types).
  - Group E (Polar): All months average below 10°C (includes ET tundra with permafrost and EF ice caps).

### 5. Climate Change Drivers and Anthropogenic Forcing
- Historical Variability: Natural climate shifts are documented through geological records, glacial advance/retreat traces, lake sediment layers, and tree rings (e.g., Pleistocene glacial epochs, medieval warm periods, and the Little Ice Age).
- Natural Climate Drivers:
  - Sunspot Activity: Cyclical variations in solar surface patches linked to climatic shifts.
  - Milankovitch Oscillations: Cyclical changes in Earth's orbital geometry, axial tilt, and wobble that alter incoming insolation.
  - Volcanism: Aerosol ejections from volcanic eruptions that temporarily reduce incoming solar radiation.
- Anthropogenic Forcing & Greenhouse Effect: The atmosphere transmits short-wave solar radiation but absorbs outgoing long-wave terrestrial radiation via **greenhouse gases (GHGs)** such as carbon dioxide (CO₂), chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and ozone (O₃). Industrial emissions and fossil fuel combustion drive modern global warming, prompting international policy frameworks like the **Kyoto Protocol (1997)**.

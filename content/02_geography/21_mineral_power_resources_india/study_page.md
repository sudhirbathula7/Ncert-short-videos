# High-Yield Revision Sheet: Mineral & Power Resources of India

# Mineral & Power Resources of India
*Subject: Geography & Resource Economics | NCERT Class 8 Ch. 3 & Class 10 | UPSC & State PSC Core*

---

### ⚡ 30-Second Snapshot
- A **mineral** is a naturally occurring substance with a definite chemical composition, while an **ore** is a rock from which minerals are commercially extracted.
- Minerals are categorized into **metallic** (ferrous containing iron, and non-ferrous) and **non-metallic** categories.
- Extraction methods depend on depth: **open-cast** for surface layers, **shaft mining** for deep deposits, and **drilling** for petroleum and natural gas.
- Conventional energy relies on firewood and **fossil fuels** (coal and petroleum), which are non-renewable and formed over millions of years.
- Non-conventional energy sources include **solar, wind, nuclear, geothermal, tidal, and biogas**, offering sustainable alternatives to fossil fuels.
- Mineral conservation requires minimizing mining waste and practicing resource recycling.

### 📐 Energy Typologies & Resource Benchmarks

- **Conventional Sources of Energy:**
  - **Coal:** Nicknamed **Buried Sunshine**; formed from ancient swamps; powers thermal electricity. Key Indian fields: Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro.
  - **Petroleum:** Nicknamed **Black Gold**; thick black liquid refined into petrol, diesel, and plastics. Key Indian fields: Digboi, Bombay High, Krishna-Godavari delta.
  - **Natural Gas:** Found alongside petroleum deposits; cleaner fuel including Compressed Natural Gas (CNG).
  - **Hydel Power:** Generates electricity from falling water; supplies roughly one-quarter of global electricity. Norway was the first country to develop hydroelectricity.
- **Non-Conventional Sources of Energy:**
  - **Solar & Wind:** Harnesses sunlight via photovoltaic cells and kinetic wind energy via wind farms.
  - **Nuclear Power:** Generated through nuclear fission of radioactive elements like uranium (Rajasthan, Jharkhand) and thorium found in Kerala monazite sands. Key stations: Tarapur, Kalpakkam, Narora, Kaiga.
  - **Geothermal Energy:** Utilizes heat from Earth interior; Indian sites include Manikaran (Himachal Pradesh) and Puga Valley (Ladakh).
  - **Tidal & Biogas:** Tidal energy harnessed via oceanic barrages in the Gulf of Kachchh; biogas produced from organic decomposition.

### ⚠️ Common UPSC Exam Traps
- **Trap 1 (Ore vs Mineral Definition):** All ores are rocks containing minerals, but not all minerals are commercial ores. Only about 100 out of 2,800+ minerals are mined commercially.
- **Trap 2 (Rock Host Environments):** Metallic minerals are predominantly found in igneous and metamorphic rock plateaus, whereas non-metallic minerals and fossil fuels occur in sedimentary strata.
- **Trap 3 (Energy Source Classification):** Firewood and fossil fuels are conventional and non-renewable, whereas solar, wind, and geothermal are non-conventional and renewable.
- **Trap 4 (Nuclear Mineral Locations):** Thorium is notably concentrated in the monazite sands of Kerala, whereas primary domestic uranium deposits are located in Rajasthan and Jharkhand.

### 📝 Prelims Practice Challenge

**Q1. Consider the following statements regarding minerals and their geological occurrence:**
1. Metallic minerals are mostly found in sedimentary rock plains and young fold mountains.
2. An ore is a rock formation from which specific minerals can be mined profitably.
3. Natural gas is exclusively found independent of petroleum deposits across all global basins.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 only
- (B) 2 only
- (C) 2 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(B) 2 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is incorrect because metallic minerals mostly concentrate in igneous and metamorphic rock plateaus, while non-metallic minerals like limestone occur in sedimentary rocks. Statement 2 is correct because an ore is specifically a rock from which minerals are mined. Statement 3 is incorrect because natural gas is typically found with petroleum deposits and is released when crude oil reaches the surface.

**Q2. Consider the following statements regarding power resources in India:**
1. Thorium, used in nuclear power generation, is abundantly concentrated in the monazite sands of Kerala.
2. Tidal energy in India is harnessed primarily through geothermal hot springs in the Puga Valley.
3. Hydroelectric power supplies a negligible fraction of global electricity compared to firewood.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 only
- (B) 1 and 3 only
- (C) 2 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(A) 1 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is correct because thorium is found in Kerala monazite sands. Statement 2 is incorrect because tidal energy is harnessed via sea barrages in the Gulf of Kachchh, whereas Puga Valley is a geothermal energy site. Statement 3 is incorrect because hydel power supplies about one-quarter of the world's electricity.

### 🖋️ Mains Practice Question (10 Marks / 150 Words)

**Question:** *"Analyze the significance of non-conventional energy sources in meeting India's future energy security demands, highlighting key geographic potentials across the country."*

#### 💡 Structured Answer Framework:
- **Introduction (25–30 words):**  
  Define the vulnerability of relying solely on conventional fossil fuels and emphasize the strategic imperative of transitioning toward renewable, non-conventional energy assets for sustainable development.
- **Body Arguments (80–90 words):**  
  - *Solar and Wind Potential:* India tropical location offers vast solar insolation potential, while coastal corridors and mountain passes support large-scale wind farm integration.
  - *Nuclear and Geothermal Resources:* Utilization of domestic uranium in Rajasthan/Jharkhand and thorium in Kerala's monazite sands, alongside geothermal extraction hubs like Manikaran and Puga Valley.
  - *Tidal and Biomass Integration:* Harnessing tidal barrages in the Gulf of Kachchh and promoting decentralized biogas digesters in rural sectors for clean cooking and organic manure.
- **Conclusion / Way Forward (25–30 words):**  
  Accelerating non-conventional energy adoption ensures long-term environmental sustainability, reduces carbon footprints, and secures national energy independence.

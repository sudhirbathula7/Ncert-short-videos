# Topic 21: Mineral & Power Resources of India - Architectural Blueprint & Pedagogical Framing

## 1. Syllabus & Exam Integration
- UPSC Prelims:
  - Geological classification of minerals into metallic (ferrous versus non-ferrous) and non-metallic categories, alongside their host rock environments.
  - Extraction methodologies ranging from open-cast mining and shaft mining to deep-well drilling and surface quarrying.
  - Global geographic distribution hotspots, including the Canadian Shield, African diamond and gold belts, and Australian bauxite reserves.
  - Conventional fossil fuel economics, including coal formation epochs, petroleum occurrence fields, and natural gas reserves.
  - Non-conventional and renewable energy harnessing infrastructure in India, covering nuclear power stations, geothermal sites, and tidal generation zones.
- UPSC Mains (GS Paper I & III - Economic & Human Geography):
  - Spatial unevenness of mineral distribution and its direct bearing on regional industrial localization and economic disparities.
  - Energy security challenges, resource transition strategies from fossil fuels to renewables, and decentralized green energy systems.
  - Environmental externalities of mining, mineral conservation ethics, and sustainable resource management frameworks.

## 2. Structural Breakdown (The 5 Core Learning Pillars)
1. Pillar 1: Mineral Classification & Geological Genesis - Defining minerals, ores, rocks, and the distinction between metallic and non-metallic deposits.
2. Pillar 2: Extraction Techniques & Global Distribution - Mining methods and continent-wise resource mapping.
3. Pillar 3: Conventional Energy Resources - Fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas) and hydroelectric power generation dynamics.
4. Pillar 4: Non-Conventional & Renewable Energy Alternatives - Solar, wind, geothermal, tidal, nuclear, and biogas energy infrastructures.
5. Pillar 5: Conservation, Sustainability & Future Pathways - The non-renewable nature of minerals and energy assets, recycling frameworks, and resource conservation.

## 3. High-Yield Data & Benchmarks
- Total Identified Mineral Types: Over 2,800 types, of which roughly 100 are classified as commercial ore minerals.
- Hydroelectricity Pioneer: Norway, recognized as the world's first country to develop hydroelectricity.
- Nuclear Energy Assets: Thorium abundance concentrated in the monazite sands of Kerala; strategic uranium reserves in Rajasthan and Jharkhand.
- Key Indian Mineral & Energy Hubs: Jharia, Raniganj, and Bokaro for coal; Digboi and Bombay High for petroleum; Manikaran and Puga Valley for geothermal energy.
- Energy Generation Milestones: Tidal energy harnessed via barrage systems, with pioneering stations globally in France and nationally in the Gulf of Kachchh.

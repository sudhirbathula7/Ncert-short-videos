## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Secondary Activities and Manufacturing
- Value Addition: Secondary economic activities transform raw natural resources into valuable commodities through manufacturing, processing, and infrastructure construction.
- Modern Characteristics: Spans specialization, mass production of standardized goods, mechanization, advanced automation, and technological innovation.
- Spatial Concentration: Modern manufacturing concentrates in tightly bounded global economic power centers covering less than ten percent of land area.

### 2. Location Factors and Classification
- Location Determinants: Factories locate to minimize costs and maximize profits based on market proximity, cheap raw materials, labor, energy, transport networks, and agglomeration economies.
- Footloose Industries: Flexible manufacturing units not tied to specific raw materials, dependent primarily on road network accessibility.
- Industry Classification: Grouped by size (cottage, small-scale, large-scale), raw inputs (agro, mineral, chemical, forest, animal), ownership (public, private, joint), and output (basic versus consumer goods).

### 3. High-Technology Industries and Technopolies
- High-Tech Features: Intensive research and development yielding advanced scientific and engineering products with a white-collar workforce dominance.
- Technopolies: Regionally concentrated, self-sustained high-tech industrial clusters such as Silicon Valley and Silicon Forest.

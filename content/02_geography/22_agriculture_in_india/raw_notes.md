## Core Factive & Conceptual Architecture

### 1. Land, Soil, and Water Context in Resource Planning
- Resource Foundation: Land, soil, water, and natural vegetation form the fundamental natural resource base that sustains agricultural systems and rural livelihoods.
- Land Utilization: Agricultural land use depends on topography, soil fertility, climate availability, and water resources.

### 2. Economic Activities and Farming Systems
- Economic Sectors: Divided into primary activities (extraction like agriculture and fishing), secondary activities (processing like manufacturing and weaving), and tertiary activities (support services like transport and banking).
- Agriculture Definition: A primary activity involving crop cultivation, horticulture, viticulture, sericulture, pisciculture, and livestock rearing. Engages about half of the global population and two-thirds of India.
- Farm System: Consists of inputs (seeds, fertilizers, machinery, labor, climate, soil), processes (plowing, sowing, irrigation, weeding, harvesting), and outputs (crops, wool, dairy, poultry).
- Farming Types:
  - Subsistence Farming: Aimed at family consumption. Includes intensive subsistence (high labor, small plots, multiple cropping of rice/wheat) and primitive subsistence (shifting cultivation or slash-and-burn like Jhumming, Milpa, Roca, Ladang; and nomadic herding).
  - Commercial Farming: Aimed at market sale. Includes commercial grain farming in temperate grasslands, mixed farming (crops plus livestock), and plantation agriculture (single-crop scale for rubber, coffee, tea).

### 3. Major Crops and Agricultural Development
- Major Crops & Requirements:
  - Rice: High temperature, humidity, rainfall, and clayey alluvial soil.
  - Wheat: Moderate temperature and rainfall, bright sunshine at harvest, well-drained loamy soil.
  - Millets: Hardy crops grown in less fertile, sandy soils with low rainfall.
  - Maize: Moderate temperature, lots of sunshine, and fertile soil.
  - Cotton: High temperature, light rainfall, frost-free days, and black/alluvial soil.
  - Jute: High temperature, heavy rainfall, and humid tropical climate.
  - Coffee and Tea: Warm or cool climates on well-drained hill slopes with abundant labor.
- Agricultural Development: Efforts to increase production through expanded area, better irrigation, high-yielding variety seeds, and mechanization to achieve food security.
- Case Studies:
  - Indian Smallholder Farm: Munna Lal in Uttar Pradesh farming about one and a half hectares with family labor, seasonal credit, and local mandi sales.
  - US Commercial Farm: Joe Horan in Iowa farming about three hundred hectares using satellite-linked machinery, scientific soil testing, and commercial business practices.

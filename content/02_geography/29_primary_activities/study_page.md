# High-Yield Revision Sheet: Primary Activities

# Primary Activities
*Subject: Geography & Economic Sectors | NCERT Class 12 Ch. 4 | UPSC & State PSC Core*

---

### ⚡ 30-Second Snapshot
- **Primary activities** are economic tasks directly dependent on the environment, utilizing natural resources like land, water, vegetation, and minerals (referred to as red-collar work).
- Early survival strategies include **hunting and gathering**, while **pastoralism** involves nomadic herding and transhumance.
- **Agricultural systems** range from primitive subsistence (shifting cultivation) and intensive wet-paddy farming to large-scale plantations and extensive mechanized grain cultivation.
- **Specialized farming** includes mixed farming, dairy operations, Mediterranean viticulture, market gardening, truck farming, factory farming, and co-operative/collective farming.
- **Mining profitability** depends on physical deposit traits and economic infrastructure, utilizing either surface open-cast or underground shaft methods.
- Global mineral production is shifting toward developing nations as advanced economies face high labor costs.

---

### 🏛️ Agricultural Typologies & Structural Grid

| Agricultural System | Core Characteristics & Methods | Global Distribution & Regional Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primitive Subsistence** | Slash-and-burn forest clearing, temporary small plots, shifting fertility | Tropical Africa, South/Central America, Southeast Asia (Jhumming in NE India, Milpa in Mexico). |
| **Intensive Subsistence** | Small landholdings, heavy family labor, manual tillage, high yield per acre | Densely populated monsoon regions of Asia (wet-paddy dominated or alternate crops). |
| **Plantation Agriculture** | Large estates, single-crop specialization, heavy capital and colonial origins | Tropical regions (tea in India/Sri Lanka, coffee in Brazil, rubber in Malaysia). |
| **Extensive Grain Farming** | Vast mechanized farms, single growing season, high yield per worker | Semi-arid mid-latitude lands (Canadian Prairies, Eurasian steppes, Argentine Pampas). |
| **Mixed Farming** | Equal emphasis on crop cultivation and animal husbandry with crop rotation | Northwestern Europe, eastern North America, Eurasian temperate zones. |
| **Dairy Farming** | Highly capital and labor-intensive milch-animal rearing near urban markets | Northwestern Europe, Canada, southeastern Australia, New Zealand. |

---

### 📐 Specialized Farming and Mining Mechanics

- **Specialized Agricultural Forms:**
  - **Mediterranean Agriculture:** Focused on viticulture (grape cultivation for wine), citrus fruits, olives, and figs in warm winter-rain regions.
  - **Market Gardening & Horticulture:** High-value vegetable, fruit, and flower production for urban markets. Includes *truck farming* (overnight transport radius) and *factory farming* (stall-reared livestock on manufactured feed).
  - **Co-operative & Collective Farming:** Voluntary resource pooling in co-ops (common in Denmark and Italy) versus socially owned collective farming (Kolkhoz in the former Soviet Union).
- **Mining Methodologies:**
  - **Surface Open-Cast Mining:** Easiest and cheapest method for shallow deposits, yielding rapid output with low overhead safety costs.
  - **Underground Shaft Mining:** Sinks vertical shafts for deep ores, requiring complex ventilation, lifts, and safety protocols against toxic gases and cave-ins.

---

### ⚠️ Common UPSC Exam Traps
- **Trap 1 (Primary Sector Definition):** Primary activities involve direct resource extraction from nature, whereas manufacturing and processing belong to the secondary sector.
- **Trap 2 (Yield Dynamics in Grain Farming):** Extensive commercial grain farming achieves low yield per acre, but high yield per person due to extreme mechanization across vast acreage.
- **Trap 3 (Shifting Cultivation Terminology):** Shifting cultivation is known by different local names globally, such as Jhumming in India, Milpa in Central America, and Ladang in Malaysia.
- **Trap 4 (Mining Cost Dynamics):** Shaft mining carries significantly higher operational risks and safety costs than open-cast surface mining due to subterranean hazards.

### 📝 Prelims Practice Challenge

**Q1. Consider the following statements regarding primary economic activities:**
1. Gathering is a high-capital, high-technology economic activity yielding large commercial surpluses.
2. Transhumance refers to the seasonal migration of herders with their livestock between lowland plains and mountain pastures.
3. Extensive commercial grain farming is characterized by very large mechanized farms with low yield per acre but high yield per person.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(B) 2 and 3 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is incorrect because gathering requires little capital and low technology, resulting in very low per-person yield and little to no surplus. Statement 2 is correct because transhumance involves seasonal vertical migration. Statement 3 is correct because extensive grain farming uses heavy mechanization across vast acreage.

**Q2. Consider the following statements regarding agricultural systems and mining:**
1. Plantation agriculture is characterized by single-crop specialization, large capital investment, and colonial origins.
2. Viticulture involves the commercial cultivation of grapes, forming a specialty of Mediterranean agriculture.
3. Open-cast mining is more hazardous and complex than underground shaft mining due to poisonous gases and cave-ins.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 and 3 only
- (C) 1 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(A) 1 and 2 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statements 1 and 2 are correct because plantations focus on single cash crops, and viticulture is grape cultivation centered around Mediterranean climates. Statement 3 is incorrect because open-cast surface mining is the easiest and cheapest method, whereas underground shaft mining carries the severe hazards of gases, fires, and cave-ins.

### 🖋️ Mains Practice Question (10 Marks / 150 Words)

**Question:** *"Examine the structural differences between subsistence agriculture and commercial agricultural systems. How do environmental and socio-economic factors shape global farming patterns?"*

#### 💡 Structured Answer Framework:
- **Introduction (25–30 words):**  
  Define primary economic activities and introduce agriculture as a diverse primary sector shaped by varying physical environments and socio-economic conditions.
- **Body Arguments (80–90 words):**  
  - *Subsistence Systems:* Characterized by small landholdings, family labor, and local consumption, including intensive wet-paddy farming and primitive shifting cultivation.
  - *Commercial Systems:* Capital-intensive, highly mechanized, and market-oriented, featuring extensive grain farms, colonial plantations, and specialized dairy operations.
  - *Environmental and Economic Drivers:* Climate, soil fertility, topography, transport infrastructure, and capital availability dictate regional agricultural choices.
- **Conclusion / Way Forward (25–30 words):**  
  Balancing agricultural productivity with environmental sustainability is vital for ensuring long-term food security and rural livelihoods.

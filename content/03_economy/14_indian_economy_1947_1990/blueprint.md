# Topic 14: Architectural Blueprint & Pedagogical Framing

## 1. Syllabus & Exam Integration
- UPSC Prelims:
  - Features of the colonial agrarian economy (Zamindari tenures, forced cash cropping, stagnant crop output).
  - Economic drain mechanics: Export surpluses drained for British home administrative charges.
  - Demographic indicators at Independence (Literacy, infant mortality, life expectancy).
  - The Mixed Economy architecture and the constitutional/institutional role of the Planning Commission (1950).
  - The four planning pillars: Growth, Modernisation, Self-Reliance, Equity.
  - Institutional land reforms: Abolition of intermediaries and land ceiling ceilings.
  - Green Revolution dynamics: HYV seed technology, inputs package, marketed surplus, and the subsidy debate.
  - Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956 (IPR 1956): Schedule classification and industrial licensing rules.
  - Import Substitution instruments: Tariffs vs. Quotas, infant industry arguments, and Karve Committee (1955) SSI reservations.
- UPSC Mains (GS Paper III - Indian Economy):
  - Colonial de-industrialisation: How the decline of indigenous handicrafts distorted India's occupational balance.
  - Assessment of the 1950–1990 planning model: Industrial diversification versus public sector inefficiencies and rent-seeking under the license permit raj.
  - Green Revolution paradox: Achieving food self-sufficiency alongside regional disparities and ecological issues.
  - The agricultural subsidy debate: Economic necessity for vulnerable tillers versus fiscal drag and natural resource depletion.

## 2. Structural Breakdown (The 5 Core Learning Pillars)
1. Pillar 1: The Colonial Inheritance (1947) — Stagnant agriculture, de-industrialisation, the export drain, and low demographic baselines.
2. Pillar 2: Planning Consensus & The Four Pillars — Choosing a mixed economy, Five Year Plans, perspective planning, and core objectives.
3. Pillar 3: Agrarian Reforms & The Green Revolution — Tenurial abolition, land ceilings, HYV packages, marketed surplus, and the subsidy debate.
4. Pillar 4: The Heavy Industrial Strategy — Commanding heights, IPR 1956 schedule classifications, industrial licensing, and the Karve Committee SSI model.
5. Pillar 5: Trade Policy & Import Substitution — Inward orientation, infant industry protection via tariffs and quotas, and structural diversification outcomes by 1990.

## 3. High-Yield Data & Benchmarks
- Demographic Profile at Independence: Literacy under 16 percent; female literacy around 7 percent; life expectancy roughly 32 years; infant mortality 218 per 1,000.
- Pre-Independence Economic Expansion: Real GDP growth below 2 percent; per capita growth around 0.5 percent per year.
- Planning Framework: Planning Commission established in 1950; IPR 1956 instituted three-tier industrial classification.
- Agricultural Employment Inelasticity: Farm employment moved from 67.5 percent in 1950 to 64.9 percent in 1990, despite agriculture's falling share of GDP.
- Industrial Structural Shift: Industry share of GDP expanded from 13 percent (1950-51) to 24.6 percent (1990-91).

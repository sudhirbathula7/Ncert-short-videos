"What happens when buyers and sellers meet in an open market? Equilibrium. It is the exact point where the quantity consumers want to buy matches what producers want to sell. At this market-clearing price, there are no unsold stockpiles and no unmet shortages."

### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: The Invisible Hand in Action
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Isometric cutaway showing a balancing scale self-adjusting between excess buyers and surplus warehouse stock. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"What if prices are off? If price is too low, shortages appear, and desperate buyers bid prices up. If price is too high, warehouses overflow, forcing sellers to slash prices. This self-correcting dynamic is Adam Smith's Invisible Hand—clearing markets without central commands."

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: Price Ceilings vs. Price Floors
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Cross-section illustration showing a legal ceiling bar below equilibrium and a legal support beam above equilibrium. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Watch this exam trap: governments often intervene. A Price Ceiling caps prices below equilibrium to make medicines affordable, but causes shortages that require rationing. A Price Floor sets minimum prices above equilibrium, like agricultural MSP, creating surpluses that the state must buy."

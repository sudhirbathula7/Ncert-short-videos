# Topic 06: Architectural Blueprint & Pedagogical Framing

## 1. Syllabus & Exam Integration
- UPSC Prelims:
  - Identification of market equilibrium conditions (intersection of DD and SS).
  - Dynamics of excess demand versus excess supply and the price-clearing mechanism.
  - Directional outcomes of single and simultaneous curve shifts on price and quantity.
  - Economic anatomy of Price Ceilings (rationing, black markets) versus Price Floors (procurement, buffer stocks).
  - Tax incidence principles based on relative elasticities of demand and supply.
- UPSC Mains (GS Paper III - Indian Economy):
  - Market distortions vs. welfare equity: Economic analysis of agricultural Minimum Support Prices (MSP) creating physical procurement and storage burdens for the state.
  - Price controls in social goods: Price ceilings on essential medicines (National List of Essential Medicines) and targeted public provisioning via PDS.
  - Tax incidence in fiscal policy: Distributional consequences of indirect taxes (GST) on essential versus luxury goods.

## 2. Structural Breakdown (The 5 Core Learning Pillars)
1. Pillar 1: Equilibrium Foundations — Market-clearing prices and intersection geometry.
2. Pillar 2: Disequilibrium Adjustments — Excess demand, excess supply, and the self-correcting price mechanism.
3. Pillar 3: Comparative Statics — Demand and supply shifts and their impact on market outcomes.
4. Pillar 4: Administered Controls — Theoretical and real-world mechanics of price ceilings and floors.
5. Pillar 5: Fiscal Distortions & Tax Incidence — Supply curve shifts, quantity shrinkage, and elasticity burdens.

## 3. High-Yield Data & Benchmarks
- Equilibrium Condition: Quantity Demanded = Quantity Supplied ($Q_d = Q_s$).
- Price Ceiling: Set strictly below equilibrium price ($P_c < p^*$), generating shortages.
- Price Floor: Set strictly above equilibrium price ($P_f > p^*$), generating surpluses.
- Long-Run Free Entry Price: $P = \min LRAC$ (Zero economic profit).

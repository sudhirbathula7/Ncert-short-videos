### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: The Indifference Curve & Tangency
**Visual**: Page-turn curl sound effect. Left side: classical Cartesian coordinate diagram showing convex Indifference Curves tangent to a downward-sloping linear budget line at point E.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Enter the Indifference Curve—bundles of goods giving equal satisfaction. Why is it convex? Because of the Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution: as you get more of one good, you surrender less of the other. Equilibrium hits where your willingness to trade exactly equals the market price ratio."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate blue highlight over "Consumer Equilibrium: MRS = Price Ratio (Budget Line Tangency)")*

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: The Demand Curve & The Linear Elasticity Trap
**Visual**: Page-turn transition. Left side: an etched linear demand curve labeled with elasticity values: infinity at the top, one at the midpoint, and zero at the base.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"From this equilibrium, we derive demand. But beware of this UPSC trap: a straight-line demand curve has a constant slope, but its elasticity changes at every single point! It is infinite at the top, exactly unitary at the midpoint, and approaches zero at the base."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate gold highlight over "Exam Trap: Constant Slope ≠ Constant Elasticity; Midpoint |Ed| = 1")*

### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: Revenue vs. Capital Outlays
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Isometric cutaway displaying two distinct conduits: one fueling daily administrative offices (Revenue) and another constructing railway corridors and bridges (Capital). Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"The budget is split in two. Revenue spending covers daily administrative running costs like salaries and subsidies, leaving no physical assets behind. Capital spending builds long-lasting infrastructure and repays debt. Borrowing for infrastructure builds the nation, but borrowing for daily consumption risks fiscal instability."

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: The Deficit Hierarchy
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Cross-section showing three balancing scales: Revenue Deficit, Fiscal Deficit (Total Borrowing), and Primary Deficit with interest payments isolated. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Here is a high-yield exam distinction: the Fiscal Deficit represents total government borrowing. Subtract interest payments on past debt, and you get the Primary Deficit. If the Primary Deficit hits zero, every rupee of new borrowing is spent just paying interest on inherited debt!"

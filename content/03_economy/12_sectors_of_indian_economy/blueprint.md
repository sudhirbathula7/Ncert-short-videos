# Topic 12: Architectural Blueprint & Pedagogical Framing

## 1. Syllabus & Exam Integration
- UPSC Prelims:
  - Sectoral classifications based on nature of activity (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary), terms of employment (Organised vs. Unorganised), and asset ownership (Public vs. Private).
  - Concepts of Disguised Unemployment and zero marginal labor productivity in agriculture.
  - India's structural divergence: Tertiary output dominance alongside primary employment dominance.
  - Key provisions of NREGA 2005 (100 days of guaranteed work, right to work, unemployment allowance).
  - Economic logic of public goods, basic utilities, and administered foodgrain operations.
- UPSC Mains (GS Paper III - Indian Economy):
  - Jobless growth: Evaluating why rapid service-led GDP expansion has not generated adequate quality employment.
  - Informalization of the labor force: Structural vulnerabilities of unorganised workers and the lack of social safety nets.
  - Agricultural labor transition: Strategies to shift disguised agricultural labor into agro-processing, rural manufacturing, and allied sectors.
  - The developmental rationale for state presence in infrastructure, health, and nutrition.

## 2. Structural Breakdown (The 5 Core Learning Pillars)
1. Pillar 1: Foundational Taxonomy — Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary distinctions, product boundaries, and sectoral interdependence.
2. Pillar 2: The Structural Anomaly — Comparing historical developmental transitions with India's service-output/agricultural-employment divergence.
3. Pillar 3: Disguised Unemployment & Job Creation — Zero marginal labor output, rural/urban underemployment, and NREGA statutory guarantees.
4. Pillar 4: Terms of Employment — Organised legal compliance versus unorganised vulnerabilities and social composition.
5. Pillar 5: Ownership Framework & Public Role — Public welfare goals versus private profit motives, foundational infrastructure, and nutritional interventions.

## 3. High-Yield Data & Benchmarks
- India's Employment Reality: More than 50 percent of the labor force remains in agriculture, producing around 25 percent of GDP.
- NREGA 2005 Guarantee: 100 days of wage employment per financial year for rural households willing to do unskilled manual work.
- Small and Marginal Farmers: Account for roughly 80 percent of rural households in India.
- Sectoral Multipliers: Industrial output grew 8-fold while jobs grew 2.5 times; service output grew 11-fold while jobs grew under 3 times.

# High-Yield Revision Sheet: Sectors of the Indian Economy

# Sectors of the Indian Economy
*Subject: Indian Economy & Development | NCERT Class 10 Ch. 2 | UPSC & State PSC Core*

---

### ⚡ 30-Second Snapshot
- Economic activities are classified by **nature of activity** (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary), **employment terms** (Organised, Unorganised), and **asset ownership** (Public, Private).
- Unlike developed economies that transitioned workers sequentially from farming to factories and then to services, India experienced an anomaly: **services dominate GDP, but agriculture still employs over half the workforce**.
- **Disguised unemployment** is common in rural fields, where extra family members appear engaged but their marginal contribution to output is zero.
- The **National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 (NREGA)** legally guarantees **100 days of unskilled wage work** annually to every rural household seeking employment.
- The **unorganised sector** employs the vast majority of Indian workers without formal contracts, paid leave, or social protections, with disadvantaged communities carrying a disproportionate burden.
- The **public sector** focuses on public welfare, investing in heavy infrastructure that private capital avoids and subsidizing essential goods like food and power.

---

### 🏛️ Sectoral Classifications: Nature, Conditions & Ownership

| Classification Basis | Category | Defining Characteristics | Real-World Economic Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nature of Activity** | **Primary Sector** | Direct harvesting or extraction of natural resources; forms the raw foundation for all output. | Agriculture, dairy farming, marine fishing, forestry, and mining operations. |
| **Nature of Activity** | **Secondary Sector** | Industrial processing and manufacturing that converts natural commodities into finished goods. | Textile mills, sugar refining plants, brick kilns, and automobile assembly. |
| **Nature of Activity** | **Tertiary Sector** | Intangible services that facilitate trade, logistics, administration, and digital communications. | Transport fleets, commercial banks, public schools, hospitals, and IT software hubs. |
| **Employment Terms** | **Organised Sector** | Registered with the state, follows statutory labor laws, provides job security, fixed hours, and pensions. | Government ministries, state-owned enterprises, and registered corporate factories. |
| **Employment Terms** | **Unorganised Sector** | Unregistered units outside regulatory oversight with no job security, paid leave, or social protections. | Daily-wage construction labor, street hawkers, domestic helpers, and marginal farmers. |
| **Asset Ownership** | **Public Sector** | State-owned capital assets operated for collective social welfare rather than corporate profits. | Indian Railways, Department of Posts, and municipal water utilities. |
| **Asset Ownership** | **Private Sector** | Privately owned capital assets operated primarily for commercial profit. | Tata Steel, Reliance Industries, and private transport operators. |

### ⚖️ The Indian Structural Anomaly & Employment Realities

- **The Output vs. Employment Mismatch:**
  - In developed nations, labor migrated steadily from agriculture to manufacturing, and later into service sectors.
  - In India, services surpassed agriculture as the largest share of GDP, but employment did not follow.
  - Industrial production expanded 8-fold while industrial jobs grew only 2.5 times.
  - Service output grew 11-fold while service jobs increased less than 3 times.
  - As a result, more than half of India's labor force remains in agriculture, generating only about a quarter of national GDP.
- **Disguised Unemployment (Zero Marginal Productivity):**
  - Occurs when more workers are engaged in an activity than necessary; removing several workers leaves total output unchanged.
  - Highly prevalent across small, rain-fed family plots where alternative jobs are scarce.
  - Also appears in urban settings among casual day-laborers, painters, and street hawkers working long hours for minimal income.
- **Policy Levers to Expand Rural Jobs:**
  - **Capital & Infrastructure:** Building canal systems, tube wells, rural roads, and cold-storage facilities.
  - **Agro-Processing:** Establishing local processing units (dal mills, honey collection, food canning) in rural belts.
  - **Statutory Guarantees:** Implementing NREGA 2005 to guarantee 100 days of wage work as a legal right, backed by an unemployment allowance if work is not provided within 15 days.

---

### ⚠️ Common UPSC Exam Traps
- **Trap 1 (Marginal Productivity in Disguised Unemployment):** Disguised unemployment does not mean workers produce negative output. It means their marginal productivity is zero: removing them from the work does not reduce overall production.
- **Trap 2 (Employment Share vs. Output Share):** The tertiary sector is India's largest contributor to national GDP, but the primary sector remains the largest employer of labor.
- **Trap 3 (Public Provision vs. Profit Motive):** Public sector enterprises exist primarily to fulfill social welfare and supply foundational infrastructure, not to maximize commercial returns.
- **Trap 4 (Nature of NREGA Work):** NREGA 2005 guarantees unskilled manual labor to rural adult household members. It does not provide skilled urban industrial jobs or white-collar employment.

### 📝 Prelims Practice Challenge

**Q1. Consider the following statements regarding disguised unemployment in an economy:**
1. It refers to a situation where the marginal physical productivity of an additional worker is zero.
2. It is confined exclusively to rural agricultural households and does not occur in urban service activities.
3. Withdrawing surplus workers from an enterprise experiencing disguised unemployment leads to an immediate decline in aggregate output.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 only
- (B) 1 and 2 only
- (C) 2 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(A) 1 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is correct because disguised unemployment occurs when extra labor adds nothing to total output (marginal productivity equals zero). Statement 2 is incorrect because underemployment also exists in urban areas among casual workers and vendors. Statement 3 is incorrect because removing surplus workers leaves total output unchanged.

**Q2. With reference to the structural transformation of the Indian economy over recent decades, consider the following statements:**
1. The service sector has overtaken the primary sector to become the largest contributor to both national GDP and total national employment.
2. The expansion of industrial output over the past three decades has outpaced the growth of industrial employment.
3. The National Rural Employment Guarantee Act guarantees wage employment exclusively to literate rural workers seeking skilled employment.

*Which of the statements given above is/are correct?*
- (A) 1 and 2 only
- (B) 2 only
- (C) 2 and 3 only
- (D) 1, 2, and 3

> **Answer:** **(B) 2 only**  
> **Explanation:** Statement 1 is incorrect because while services generate the largest share of GDP, agriculture continues to employ the largest share of the workforce. Statement 2 is correct because industrial output grew 8-fold while industrial jobs grew only 2.5 times. Statement 3 is incorrect because NREGA 2005 guarantees unskilled manual labor to any rural adult willing to work, regardless of literacy.

### 🖋️ Mains Practice Question (10 Marks / 150 Words)

**Question:** *"Unlike advanced economies, India's structural transformation bypassed manufacturing-led job creation, moving directly from agriculture to services in GDP contribution. Discuss the socio-economic implications of this structural mismatch."*

#### 💡 Structured Answer Framework:
- **Introduction (25–30 words):**  
  Explain that classical structural transformation moves labor from agriculture to manufacturing and then to services. India followed an unconventional path where services grew to dominate GDP while agriculture continued to employ over half the workforce.
- **Body Arguments (80–90 words):**  
  - *Underemployment & Low Incomes:* Retaining half the labor force in agriculture to generate only a quarter of GDP drives widespread disguised unemployment, depresses rural wages, and widens the rural-urban income divide.
  - *Dualism in Services:* Service sector growth has been concentrated in skill-intensive fields (IT, finance) that absorb relatively little unskilled labor, forcing excess workers into low-paying, informal tertiary jobs (street vending, casual delivery work).
  - *Missing Industrial Core:* The lack of labor-intensive manufacturing prevents the economy from absorbing surplus agricultural labor into formal, productive factory jobs with legal protections and social security.
- **Conclusion / Way Forward (25–30 words):**  
  Balancing India's structural transformation requires expanding labor-intensive manufacturing, developing rural agro-processing networks, and supporting micro-enterprises to productively absorb surplus labor.

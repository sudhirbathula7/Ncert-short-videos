### [0:00 - 0:14] Page 1: Why Barter Failed & Money Took Over
**Visual**: Clean off-white canvas. Left 60%: Architectural isometric cutaway of an ancient village market stall failing to trade grain for cattle, transitioning into a modern central bank issuing currency. Right 40%: Structured editorial typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Ever tried trading sacks of wheat for a cow? Barter collapses without a double coincidence of wants. Money solves this by acting as a universal medium of exchange, a standard unit of account, and a durable store of value. Modern paper notes carry no intrinsic value, but sovereign legal backing makes them universally accepted."  

---

### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: Fractional Banking & The Money Multiplier
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Isometric cutaway of a commercial bank vault reserving cash and feeding loans into another banking ledger in a continuous loop. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"How do commercial banks create money? Through fractional reserve banking. When you deposit cash, the bank reserves a legal percentage and lends out the rest. That loan becomes a new deposit elsewhere, multiplying through the economy. The money multiplier equals one divided by the Cash Reserve Ratio."  

---

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: The Credit Trap vs. Self-Help Groups
**Visual**: Seamless transition. Left 60%: Cross-section showing a village moneylender charging high interest versus a circle of rural women pooling savings with a bank passbook. Right 40%: Formatted typography card.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Credit can build a business or trigger a debt trap. Poorer households often lack the land titles and collateral that commercial banks require, driving them toward predatory moneylenders. That is where Self-Help Groups step in: pooling small savings and using collective peer guarantees to secure collateral-free bank loans."

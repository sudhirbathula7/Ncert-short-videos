# Topic 15: Architectural Blueprint & Pedagogical Framing

## 1. Syllabus & Exam Integration
- UPSC Prelims:
  - Macroeconomic roots of the 1991 BoP crisis: Fiscal deficit spillovers, foreign exchange reserve depletion below two weeks of imports.
  - World Bank (IBRD) and IMF loan conditions: Structural Adjustment Programme.
  - The distinction between short-term Stabilisation Measures and long-term Structural Reform Measures.
  - Industrial deregulation: Retained licensing exceptions, dereservation of small-scale industries, and removal of MRTP limits.
  - Financial sector reforms: Shift in the RBI's role from regulator to facilitator, 74 percent FDI cap in private banking, FII entry rules.
  - Currency adjustments: The 1991 two-step rupee devaluation and transition to managed floating exchange rates.
  - GATT to WTO transition (1995) and the core debate over advanced-economy agricultural subsidies.
  - Public sector reforms: Disinvestment classification (Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna tiers).
- UPSC Mains (GS Paper III - Indian Economy):
  - Jobless growth: Evaluating why service-led post-1991 growth failed to generate adequate formal manufacturing jobs.
  - Neglect of agriculture: Decline in public capital formation in irrigation and rural infrastructure during the reform era.
  - Disinvestment policy: Strategic sale of loss-making public enterprises versus selling profitable blue-chip assets.
  - Impact on domestic industry: Small-scale enterprise vulnerabilities facing international import competition.

## 2. Structural Breakdown (The 5 Core Learning Pillars)
1. Pillar 1: The 1991 Crisis & The Dual Reform Strategy — BoP emergency, foreign exchange reserve collapse, IMF-World Bank conditions, and stabilization vs. structural reforms.
2. Pillar 2: Industrial & Financial Liberalisation — Dismantling the License Raj, RBI's role transition, private bank entry, and tax rate rationalization.
3. Pillar 3: Trade & Foreign Exchange Overhaul — Currency devaluation, tariff cuts, quota removals, and moving from import substitution to export orientation.
4. Pillar 4: Privatisation & PSE Commercialization — Equity disinvestment, corporate governance, and Maharatna/Navratna autonomy tiers.
5. Pillar 5: Globalisation, Outsourcing & Sectoral Outcomes — Rise of IT-BPO hubs, WTO multilateral debates, agricultural slowdown, and foreign investment surges.

## 3. High-Yield Conversion Rules & Formulas
- Balance of Payments Stabilisation: Two-Step Currency Devaluation (1991) + Transition to Market Exchange Rate
- Private Bank Foreign Equity Cap: Up to 74 percent
- Core Retained Public Sector Areas: Atomic energy and core railway operations
- WTO Foundation: January 1, 1995 (Replacing GATT 1948)
- Foreign Capital Expansion: FDI + FII surged from roughly 100 million US dollars (1990-91) to global-scale inflows

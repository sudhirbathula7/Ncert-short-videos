# Topic 02: Architectural Blueprint & Pedagogical Framing

## 1. Syllabus & Exam Integration
- **UPSC Prelims**:
  - Distinctions between Market vs. Non-market activities in National Income.
  - The 5 sources of human capital formation according to NCERT.
  - Structural comparison: Physical Capital vs. Human Capital (externalities, separability).
  - Classifications of unemployment: Disguised ($MP_L = 0$) vs. Seasonal vs. Educated.
  - Institutional mandates: UGC, AICTE, NCERT, ICMR, NMC.
- **UPSC Mains (GS Paper II & III - Social Justice & Economy)**:
  - "Demographic Dividend vs. Demographic Disaster": The role of human capital formation in converting population size into an economic asset.
  - "Human Capital vs. Human Development": Critique of growth-centric policies using Amartya Sen's Capability Approach.
  - Structural bottlenecks in Indian education spending: Aggregate allocation trends, elementary vs. tertiary per-capita skew.
  - Public goods nature of health and education justifying state intervention over unrestrained privatization.

## 2. Structural Breakdown (The 5 Core Learning Pillars)
1. **Pillar 1: Paradigm Shift** — Reframing population from liability to productive economic asset.
2. **Pillar 2: The 5 Engines of Human Capital** — Education, health, training, migration, and information.
3. **Pillar 3: The Comparative Matrix** — Physical vs. Human Capital (Private vs. Social externalities).
4. **Pillar 4: Instrumental vs. Intrinsic Value** — Human Capital vs. Human Development paradigms.
5. **Pillar 5: Market Failures & State Mandate** — Information asymmetry, irreversibility, and regulatory architecture.

## 3. High-Yield Data & Definitions
- **Human Capital**: Stock of skill, knowledge, and health embodied in an economy's population.
- **Disguised Unemployment**: Scenario where surplus labor withdrawal leaves aggregate output unchanged ($MP_L = 0$).
- **Tertiary vs. Elementary Financing**: Elementary claims the largest aggregate share of budget; tertiary receives higher *per-student* allocation.

### [0:00 - 0:14] Page 1: The Factory Floor Dilemma
**Visual**: Clean off-white archival textbook page. Left side: classical etching of a 19th-century workshop showing one industrial steam loom surrounded by workers. Right side empty warm ivory paper.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"What happens when you keep adding workers to a factory with fixed machinery? At first, output explodes. Workers specialize, and the idle machinery is fully utilized. But soon, floor space fills up. Workers start sharing tools, and the extra output contributed by each new hire begins to plunge."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate yellow highlight over "Law of Variable Proportions: Output First Rises, Then Congests Fixed Plant")*

### [0:14 - 0:28] Page 2: The Three Phases & Disguised Unemployment
**Visual**: Page-turn curl sound effect. Left side: an etched diagram showing a rising total product line flattening out and falling, beside crowded farm laborers.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"This is the Law of Variable Proportions. In Phase One, productivity climbs. In Phase Two, returns diminish. In Phase Three, crowding turns extra output negative! This is why Indian agriculture suffers from disguised unemployment: too many people working fixed family land, adding zero real harvest."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate blue highlight over "Phase 3: Marginal Output Drops to Zero; Disguised Agrarian Unemployment")*

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: The U-Shaped Cost Curve Secret
**Visual**: Page-turn transition. Left side: clean ink drawings of U-shaped cost curves, highlighting the Marginal Cost line cutting through the lowest point of Average Total Cost.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Now look at factory costs. Overhead rent spreads out over more units, pulling average fixed costs down forever. But because labor eventually congests, average variable costs curve back upward. The result? A U-shaped average cost curve, sliced cleanly through its lowest point by Marginal Cost."

### [0:00 - 0:15] Page 1: The Core Trap — Is Development Universal?
**Visual**: Antique book page, left side features an ink cross-section comparing a modern mega-dam with submerged tribal forest villages. Right side blank ivory paper.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Does economic development mean the same thing to everyone? Not at all. What spells progress for an urban industrialist—like a massive hydroelectric dam—can mean total destruction, displacement, and lost heritage for an indigenous forest community. Income matters, but real development demands dignity, security, and freedom."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate yellow highlight over "Conflicting Goals: Dam vs Forest Displacement")*

### [0:15 - 0:28] Page 2: The Fallacy of Averages
**Visual**: Page-turn curl sound effect. Left side shows two balance scales weighing identical stacks of gold coins, but one scale has equal weights while the other has one giant weight crushing four tiny ones.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"The World Bank compares nations using Per Capita Income. But averages hide inequality. Two countries can have the exact same average income—one where every citizen lives comfortably, and another where one percent hoards all the wealth while the rest starve."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate blue highlight over "Per Capita Income = Total Income ÷ Population; Hides Inequality")*

---

### [0:28 - 0:42] Page 3: The Punjab-Kerala Paradox
**Visual**: Page-turn transition. Left side shows an antique medical infant scale beside a textbook and blackboard, contrast-mapped against a rupee bag.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"Look at India's own data: Punjab has a significantly higher per capita income than Kerala. Yet, Kerala’s Infant Mortality Rate is four times lower, and its literacy is drastically higher. Why? Because money in your pocket cannot buy a pathogen-free environment, quality healthcare, or universal public schools."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate gold highlight over "Kerala: IMR 11 vs Punjab: 49; Public Facilities Matter")*

### [0:42 - 0:58] Page 4: Human Development & The Sustainability Limit
**Visual**: Page-turn transition. Left side features a vintage global map with Sri Lanka highlighted above India, alongside a cracked agricultural well running dry.  
**Audio (Voiceover)**:  
"That’s why the UNDP's Human Development Index looks beyond cash—measuring health, education, and living standards. Sri Lanka beats India here. And remember: if growth drains one-third of our groundwater reserves, it isn't progress—it's borrowed time. Comment 'GROWTH' for the complete Cornell revision sheet."  
*(Right-Side Text Overlay: Animate yellow highlight over "UNDP HDI: Health + Education + Income; Save Groundwater")*
